For my mechanic that I made, I tried to make something loosely based off of the parrying system in Sekiro. Although it could use a lot of improvement, I think something that is very bare-bones is now created. A projectile is fired towards the player repeatedly, and they must press spacebar in time to parry it. If they do, they turn blue and make a metal sound. If not, they turn red and there is a buzzer sound.
I used an interface called IParryable, that assists in the getting hit by the projectile. It is called in projectile parry, then, if the player is parrying, they succeed, if not, then they are hit. I used deltatime both for the movement of the projectile, and for the spawning of projectiles for the player to parry. Most of my initialization is early, i.e. getting the components of rigidbody or audiosource, for example, and then I used a late initialization (start) in projectile spawner to make sure it didn't spawn something immediately.
I don't think the scope was changed particularly much. The only thing I cut out was considering making the firing of a projectile random so there wasn't as much rhythm to it, and the player really had to react. But for this, I just wanted to make sure that you could even parry the projectile in the first place, as I had to do some research on things I wasn't too familiar with. And, after creating the project, I realized everything in Unity was not saved properly, meaning I almost had to do the entire prototype over again. Thankfully, I still had the hardest part- the scripts, so I just had to rebuild the scene itself in Unity (this does mean that the showcase video was from the original version, although very little changed.)
